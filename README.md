### Project 1: Ịmụ-Asụsụ

#### PROBLEM STATEMENT

The histories and cultures of the Global South are under-represented in the digital record, and much of what _has_ been documented sits outside the communities it describes — held in foreign archives and museums, framed from an outside perspective, or carried in oral traditions that are disappearing faster than they are being recorded. The institutions positioned to close this gap are underfunded, fragmented, and rarely built around the people whose history is at stake.

#### VISION STATEMENT

A world where a community's history is recorded by the people who live it, reachable by anyone who wants it, and taught through materials worth learning from.

Ịmụ-Asụsụ is how we start building that record: a platform that holds contributors to scholarly standards, makes collaboration the default rather than the exception, and treats the design of the experience as part of the preservation.

#### HOW IT WORKS

Ịmụ-Asụsụ turns that vision into three commitments.

**Every claim carries its provenance.** Entries, figures, and lexicon entries are each tied to the sources that back them, so a reader can always ask where something came from — and a curator can always answer.

**History is a graph, not a list.** Places and peoples nest, entries relate to each other, and figures carry genealogy and succession — so a reader can arrive at any point and follow the connections outward. A list has to rank what matters before the reader arrives; a graph lets significance follow the question being asked — so a lineage or a founding that an outside survey would footnote can sit at the center.

**Contributors write for learners, not for an archive.** Manuscripts are authored, shared, forked, and annotated in the open, so what gets preserved is already teachable.
